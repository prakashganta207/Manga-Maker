Every evening for forty years, Grandpa Isamu had climbed the hundred steps of the old lighthouse to light the lamp. Tonight his granddaughter Hana climbed them alone.
The room at the top was quiet. His reading glasses still lay folded beside the logbook, as if he had only stepped out for tea.
Hana opened the logbook. On the last page, in his shaky handwriting, was a note addressed to her.
"If you are reading this, the light is yours now. Don't be afraid of the dark, Hana. The dark is how the ships find home."
Her hands trembled. She pressed the book to her chest and cried for the first time since the funeral.
Outside, the fog rolled in thick and grey. Somewhere out at sea, a ship's horn called, low and lost.
Hana wiped her eyes, struck a match, and lit the great lamp. The beam swept across the water.
The horn called again, closer now, answering the light. Hana smiled through her tears. "Welcome home," she said softly.
