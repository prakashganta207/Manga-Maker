Three days later, the river still glowed at night. Aya waited on the rooftop of the old clock tower, her courier bag empty for the first time in years.
Kenji climbed up the rusted ladder, a paper bag of warm buns in one hand. "Peace offering," he said.
"You owe me more than buns," Aya said, but she took one anyway.
Below them, a drone hovered over the water and suddenly fell, its lights blinking out. Then another. Then ten more, raining into the river.
Aya grabbed Kenji's collar. "What was in that box?"
Kenji looked at the falling drones and swallowed. "A key. It switches off every drone in the city."
A siren howled from the east. Black trucks rolled across the bridge, and men in grey coats pointed up at the clock tower.
Aya pulled her goggles down. "Then we run," she said, and for the first time she reached back for his hand.
